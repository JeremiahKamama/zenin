# Watchlists and research

Watchlists and research work together: a watchlist tells Zenin what to watch, and research explains why it matters to your portfolio.

## Adding and importing assets

From **Watchlist** you can:

- **Search** by symbol (`AAPL`, `BTC`, `SPY`, `EUR/USD`) and add.
- **Import** a list from CSV/TSV, spreadsheet, platform text, or document workflows.
- Organize items into **categorized watchlists**.

## Alert context

Turn on **alerts** for watched items where your plan allows. Alerts are tied to the instrument and surface in Notifications.

## Asset research

Open an asset from Watchlist, Portfolio, Home, or an Intelligence surface to launch **Research**. Research brings together:

- company, commodity, macro, or ETF context depending on the instrument;
- portfolio-aware actions (add to watchlist, open in portfolio, start a journal entry);
- related-market handoffs.

## Comparison

Use **compare** to weigh an asset against a peer (for example, one ETF against another). ETF research shows fund-focused fields and comparisons; stock-only company metrics are not presented as ETF data. FX-pair research distinguishes a tradable pair from a standalone currency entity.

## Moving research into a decision or journal

Research is most useful when it becomes a recorded decision:

1. Open the asset and review the relevant context.
2. Use a portfolio-aware action to **start a decision** or **journal entry**.
3. Paste or summarize the rationale so the decision and the outcome can be reviewed later.

This closes the loop from market signal to documented decision.

## What this does / does not do

**What this does**

- Lets you track multi-asset watchlists and import them in bulk.
- Opens contextual research tied to your portfolio.
- Hands research off to a decision or journal workflow.

**What it does not do**

- It does not guarantee live prices for every instrument — coverage is provider-dependent.
- It does not treat a currency code as a tradable instrument with fabricated prices.
- It does not auto-create journal entries; you choose to record them.

**Data source and freshness**

Watchlist items are user-defined. Research data comes from the configured providers (company/fundamentals, market data, macro, commodities) and may be current, delayed, stale, or reference-only by instrument and provider.
